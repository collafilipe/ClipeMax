News sources: NPR (via Michigan Public), Tubefilter, Dexerto, win.gg, Agência Senado. ## 1. YouTube Partner Program requirements for Shorts, revenue sharing mechanics, and RPM ranges (global and Brazil)

### Takeaway
Until 31 Jan 2027 a Shorts-only channel needs 1,000 subscribers + 10M qualified public Shorts views in 90 days to share ad revenue; from 1 Feb 2027 new applicants need 20M views in 90 days and every partner needs 10M views per rolling 90 days to keep earning from the Shorts Creator Pool. Creators keep 45% of their allocated pool share, and reported Shorts RPMs are very low (about US$0.01-0.05 per 1,000 views for gaming/entertainment; about US$0.045 for Brazilian audiences), so Shorts ad revenue alone is a weak business for a clips channel.

### Cited Findings
- Current (2026) full ads + Premium revenue sharing threshold: 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days; the creator must also accept the Shorts Monetization Module to earn from Shorts. — [vidIQ, YouTube Shorts Monetization 2026](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-monetization/)
- Early-access tier (unchanged in 2027): 500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 watch hours (12 months) or 3M Shorts views (90 days). It unlocks fan-funding features (memberships, Super Chat, Super Thanks, some Shopping) but NOT Shorts Feed ads, Watch Page ads or Premium revenue sharing; availability varies by country. — [vidIQ, YPP changes 2027](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- YouTube announced on 10 Aug 2026 that from 1 Feb 2027 new applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 watch hours (12 months) or 20M qualified Shorts views (90 days). vidIQ links YouTube's own announcement (https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/) and help guide (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en); those two primary pages were not fetched by me. — [vidIQ, YPP changes 2027](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- From 1 Feb 2027 a creator needs 10M qualified Shorts views over the previous 90 days to earn from the Shorts Creator Pool; a channel below that stays in YPP and keeps long-form earnings, but Shorts pool earnings pause until the threshold is met again. — [vidIQ, YPP changes 2027](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/); corroborated by [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/industry-briefing-news/youtube-sets-10-million-shorts-views-for-monetisation-from-2027-157245.html)
- Existing partners keep YPP status but from 1 Feb 2027 face an activity test (any one of: 1,000 watch hours in 365 days; 1M qualified Shorts views in 90 days; two long-form or five Shorts uploads every 90 days) and must accept updated monetization modules by 31 Jan 2027 or earnings from those features stop. — [vidIQ, YPP changes 2027](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- New from 1 Feb 2027: when an advertiser targets five or fewer channels with Shorts ads, eligible creators get a direct 45% share of that ad's revenue on top of the pool. — [vidIQ, YPP changes 2027](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- Qualified Shorts views must come from public Shorts in the Shorts Feed; private/unlisted/deleted Shorts, ad-campaign views and image posts do not count. — [vidIQ, YPP changes 2027](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- Official mechanics: (1) Shorts Feed ad revenue is pooled monthly; (2) the Creator Pool is calculated from engaged views and music usage (a Short with one music track sends half its associated revenue to music partners, two tracks two thirds; no music = 100% to the Creator Pool); (3) the pool is allocated by each creator's share of engaged views among monetizing creators in each country; (4) "Monetizing creators will keep 45% of their allocated revenue, regardless if music was used or not." — [YouTube Help, Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Official list of views that are NOT counted for Shorts payment: non-original Shorts such as "unedited clips from others' movies or TV shows" and "reuploading other creators' content from YouTube or other platform"; compilations with no original content added; artificial/fake views; views of Shorts that are not advertiser-friendly. — [YouTube Help, Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Only music from YouTube's music-industry partners (or Dream Track) reduces the Creator Pool; other third-party content gets no credit from the pool "even when a Content ID monetize policy is set on that content." — [YouTube Help, Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- RPM estimates (third-party, low confidence): direct Shorts ad share about US$0.01-0.05 per 1,000 views — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-monetization/); gaming/entertainment Shorts typically US$0.01-0.03 — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-pay-rate-2026); Brazil Shorts RPM about US$0.045 — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/what-rpm-can-you-expect-from-shorts-in-2026); Brazil range R$0.10-1.50 (US$0.02-0.28) per 1,000 — [FluxNote Brazil](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-rpm-brazil-2026) (unsourced, conflicts with AIR on the upper end).
- Premium Lite is expanding to all Premium countries; its creator pool is 60% of net subscription revenue (vs 30% for standard Premium per vidIQ's summary), with 45% of Shorts allocations going to creators. — [vidIQ, YPP changes 2027](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic from the cited RPMs: at US$0.03-0.045 per 1,000 views, 10M Shorts views (the minimum quarterly volume just to stay eligible from Feb 2027) yields roughly US$300-450 per quarter. Shorts ad revenue is therefore a side income; paid clipping campaigns at about US$1 per 1,000 views (section 5) pay on the order of 20-30x more per view.
- Because pool allocation is per country of viewer, a Portuguese-language clips channel with a Brazilian audience will sit near the Brazil figure rather than US-level RPMs.
- A new channel starting in Nov 2026 has under three months to qualify under the old 10M/90-day entry bar before it doubles to 20M; in practice a fresh clips channel should assume the 2027 rules.
- Since clips are short, the long-form route (watch hours) only helps if the channel also posts horizontal compilations/long videos; Shorts Feed watch time does not count toward watch hours.

### Gaps
- No reliable, primary-sourced Shorts RPM specifically for gaming clips viewed in Brazil; all figures are blog estimates that disagree.
- YouTube's own blog post and help article for the 2027 changes were not read directly (only via vidIQ, exchange4media and Portuguese coverage such as Canaltech/TNH1 appearing in search results).
- vidIQ notes YouTube has not clarified how early-access (500-sub) members or applications pending on 1 Feb 2027 are treated.

## 2. YouTube "reused content" and "inauthentic content" policies: wording, 2025-2026 updates, and what transformation is sufficient

### Takeaway
YouTube's reused-content policy is independent of copyright: a clips channel can be refused or removed from monetization even with the streamer's permission unless each video adds visible original value (commentary, narrative, substantive editing). Raw or lightly captioned clips "edited together with little or no narrative" are the textbook non-monetizable example, and a templated high-volume output additionally risks the "inauthentic / generic or repetitive content" rule; enforcement is channel-wide.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: "Reused content refers to channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source" without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational/entertainment value; the test is whether the channel adds meaningful original value, not copyright status. — [YouTube Help, channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Listed as allowed to monetize: clips used for critical review; reaction videos that comment on the original; edited footage with added storyline and commentary; content where the uploader is visible or explains how they added to it. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Listed as not allowed: clips from a favorite show "edited together with little or no narrative"; content whose views come mostly from non-verbal reactions "without added voice commentary"; compilations of other artists' songs even with permission. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- The policy applies even with the original creator's permission; copyright rules apply separately. If reused videos violate the policy, or the reviewer cannot tell the channel made the content, monetization can be removed from the entire channel. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- The Portuguese help page words the standard as reuse "sem adicionar comentários originais significativos", and lists promoting someone else's content, even with permission, among non-qualifying examples (as quoted by a Brazilian vendor blog citing support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=pt-BR). — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/how-to-make-money-clipping-2026)
- 15 Jul 2025 changelog: the "repetitious content" policy was clarified and renamed "inauthentic content" (mass-produced or repetitive videos); YouTube states the reused content policy did not change. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- July 2026: YouTube reworked the guidance into three categories — "Generic or Repetitive Content" (content that "looks like it's made with a template", little commentary, minimal variation), "Unsatisfying or Off-putting Content" (new label: manipulative formulas, interchangeable formats, shock content) and AI personas giving expert advice on sensitive topics. Tubefilter stresses this is a communications update, not a new rule. — [Tubefilter, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)
- The current help page has no section literally titled "Inauthentic content"; the nearest sections are "Generic or Repetitive Content" (not allowed: slideshows, templated storylines, scrolling text with little narrative, template-based AI content suggesting mass production; allowed: same intro/outro with varying main content) and "Unsatisfying or Off-putting Content". — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- On Shorts specifically, views on "unedited clips" of others' content, reuploads from YouTube or other platforms, and compilations with no original content are excluded from payment even on a channel that is in YPP. — [YouTube Help, Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- Third-party guides disagree on compilations: some say commentary-driven edits still earn, others say compilations without substantial commentary are often demonetized. — [vidIQ reused content guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected)

### Inferences
- For a GTA-stream clips channel the monetizable pattern implied by the official examples is: own voice-over or on-screen commentary, a narrative frame (setup/context/payoff), meaningful editing beyond cropping to 9:16 and auto-captions, and visible variation between videos. Captions, zoom cuts and a hook title alone match the "little or no narrative" example rather than the allowed ones.
- Authorization from the streamer solves copyright but not YPP eligibility; the two must be handled separately.
- A channel that is refused YPP for reused content can still earn from paid clipping campaigns, since those pay off-platform per view (section 5).

### Gaps
- No direct, dated statement from a named YouTube staff member (Creator Insider / TeamYouTube) specifically about streamer-clip channels was retrieved; only the policy examples and trade-press paraphrases.
- YouTube does not publish any quantitative threshold for "significant" commentary or editing.

## 3. TikTok Creator Rewards Program in 2026: eligibility, Brazil, 1-minute rule, originality, and treatment of third-party stream clips

### Takeaway
TikTok has dedicated Creator Rewards Program terms for Brazil (18+, 10,000 followers, 100,000 views in 30 days, personal account, videos of at least 1 minute, creator warrants ownership and originality, US$50 minimum paid via PayPal/Hyperwallet). Clips of someone else's stream are the kind of content widely reported as flagged "unoriginal" and excluded from rewards, so the program is a poor fit for a pure clips account unless content is heavily transformed.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok publishes program terms specific to Brazil ("tiktok-creator-rewards-program-br"), last updated Apr 2025; the page itself does not list countries, requiring only legal residence in "the country where the Program is made available." — [TikTok CRP terms (Brazil)](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-br/en)
- Eligibility: "18 years of age or older or the age of majority"; at least 10,000 "authentic followers"; at least 100,000 "authentic total video views" in the 30 days before applying; account in good standing; excludes Business/Organization accounts and government/political accounts; payment account in the creator's own name. — [TikTok CRP terms (Brazil)](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-br/en)
- Eligible videos must have "a duration of at least 1 minute" and be publicly posted; the creator warrants "its ownership and originality". The terms do not define "original" and do not explicitly address duets, stitches or reposts. — [TikTok CRP terms (Brazil)](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-br/en)
- Rewards are based on "total legitimate video views" and other engagement signals; artificial, paid/incentivized or self-generated metrics are excluded; TikTok may impose a "monthly or annual cap". TikTok may mute, remove or disqualify videos with "unauthorized uses of music". — [TikTok CRP terms (Brazil)](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-br/en)
- Payment: estimates shown in USD; minimum threshold "$50.00 USD"; paid "on the 15th of the month" for the prior month's rewards via PayPal/Hyperwallet; creator bears transfer fees. Brazil-specific: conversion to Real "may be subject to the Tax on Financial Operations (IOF)"; the creator is "solely responsible" for taxes and certifies non-US tax status. — [TikTok CRP terms (Brazil)](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-br/en)
- Third-party guides list Brazil among eligible countries (alongside US, UK, Germany, France, Japan, South Korea) but note TikTok does not publish a definitive list. — [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/data/reference/creator-fund-countries/); [shortsfast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-eligibility-2026/)
- Brazilian creators reportedly see RPMs at the low end of the global range (third-party, no hard number retrieved). — [ttcalculator Brazil](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/tiktok-pay-brazil-earnings/)
- Third-party reports (not official): re-uploading viral clips, movie scenes, unedited podcast audio or TV highlights "will get you flagged fast", and gaming clips/recaps are often hit even when edited together; flags pause earnings and repeated flags (often cited as three, per creator anecdotes) risk disqualification. — [influenceflow](https://influenceflow.io/resources/why-is-my-tiktok-unoriginal-content-flag-appearing-and-how-to-fix-it/); [socialboostdigital](https://socialboostdigital.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-unoriginal-content-flag)
- TikTok reportedly tightened enforcement on unoriginal content from 15 Sep 2025. — [BigSeller](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/3778/tiktok-unoriginal-content.htm)
- Creators report disqualifications with no clear explanation or working appeal, including original creators wrongly flagged. — [Baller Alert](https://balleralert.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-disqualification/); [techissuestoday](https://techissuestoday.com/tiktok-creators-cry-foul-as-viral-videos-get-disqualified-before-payday)
- A clipping-tool guide advises clippers to plan TikTok clip income without counting on Creator Rewards and recommends short (15-34 s) streamer clips, which are below the 1-minute eligibility floor anyway. — [OpenClip, Clipping on TikTok](https://openclip.app/guides/clipping-on-tiktok)
- Other routes mentioned for clippers in Brazil: TikTok Shop / affiliate links, selling ads on the clip channel, per-view campaigns. — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/how-to-make-money-clipping-2026)

### Inferences
- The 1-minute minimum conflicts with the typical viral clip length, and the originality warranty conflicts with third-party footage; a clips account would realistically monetize TikTok through external campaigns, affiliate/TikTok Shop, LIVE gifts or sponsorships rather than Creator Rewards.
- Because the account must be a personal (not Business) account in the creator's own name with a matching payment account, running many clip accounts under one person may not multiply Creator Rewards eligibility.

### Gaps
- TikTok's official definition of "original content"/"unoriginal content" (Creator Academy / support page) was not retrieved; treatment of stream clips rests on third-party reports.
- No reliable RPM figure for Creator Rewards in Brazil.
- Details of other TikTok monetization availability in Brazil (Subscriptions, LIVE gifts thresholds, TikTok Shop affiliate requirements) were not verified from official pages.

## 4. Twitch, Kick and YouTube terms on third-party re-uploads; what streamers allow

### Takeaway
None of the platform licences gives a third-party clipper the right to re-upload a stream elsewhere: the streamer keeps ownership and licenses the platform, so off-platform clipping depends on the streamer's permission or tolerance. In practice many large streamers actively want clips and run Discord-based clipper programs with rules (mandatory tag/watermark, submission process), and Kick formalized a platform-level clipping program in late 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Twitch: streamers are responsible for what they stream and upload, including clips; rights holders (or agents) can send DMCA notifications, after which Twitch disables the content and notifies the streamer. — [Twitch Creator Camp](https://twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/paths/copyrights-and-your-channel/why-are-copyright-laws-important-for-me-to-think-about-when-i-stream); [Twitch DMCA guidelines](https://tr.twitch.tv/p/en/legal/dmca-guidelines)
- Twitch users grant Twitch a broad, royalty-free, sublicensable, transferable, perpetual worldwide licence over their content (third-party summary of the ToS; the licence runs to Twitch, not to other users for off-platform use). — [ConductAtlas, Twitch ToS](https://conductatlas.com/platform/twitch/twitch-terms-of-service/perpetual-user-content-license/)
- Since 2020 Twitch deletes clips hit by DMCA notices and issues strikes; Audible Magic scanning was extended to Clips (muting is not a strike). Historic 2019/2020 cases show accounts that merely created clips containing copyrighted content receiving penalties. (Older reporting; may be superseded in details.) — [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2020/06/08/twitch-dmca-takedowns-tos-changes/); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/multiple-twitch-users-given-24-hour-bans-for-clipping-copyrighted-content-112314/)
- Kick: users grant Kick "a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, sublicensable and transferable license" over User Content, and Kick "claims no ownership rights" (third-party provision records of the Kick ToS). — [ConductAtlas, Kick ToS](https://conductatlas.com/platform/kick/kick-terms-of-service/user-content-license/)
- Open Terms Archive noted in Sep 2025 that Kick added terms governing commercialization of platform content and tools. — [Open Terms Archive](https://opentermsarchive.org/en/memos/kick-asserts-control-over-commercialization-and-digital-content/)
- Kick made its clipping program official (article dated 11 Dec 2025): any Kick streamer can apply through a portal in the Kick Discord with no threshold; clippers monitor streams and post clips to social media; Kick claimed the initiative drove over 3 billion clip views in a single September; Adin Ross had earlier said Kick was paying clippers. — [win.gg](https://win.gg/kick-clipping-program/)
- Brazilian practical guidance: "Muitos streamers grandes têm programa de clipador no Discord, com regras, tag obrigatória e forma de envio"; check pinned messages, channel/video descriptions and panels under the stream on Twitch/Kick, and keep a dated screenshot of any public authorization. — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/ask-permission-to-clip-streamer-2026)
- The streamer can only authorize what is theirs: music, film excerpts and football broadcasts inside the stream are not cleared by the streamer's permission. — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/ask-permission-to-clip-streamer-2026)
- Clips outperform the source: Hasan Piker's livestreams average about 33,000 viewers while his average clip gets more than 700,000 views, which is why streamers tolerate or pay for clipping. — [NPR via Michigan Public, 12 May 2026](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2026-05-12/the-clipping-economy-how-short-form-video-clippers-are-overrunning-the-internet)

### Inferences
- The safest legal basis for a clips channel is a documented permission: either a streamer's published clipper-program rules or a written one-to-one agreement; "everyone does it" tolerance can be withdrawn by a takedown at any time.
- Kick-based streamers are structurally the most clipper-friendly targets because the platform itself subsidizes clip distribution with Kick branding on the clips.

### Gaps
- The live text of Twitch's and Kick's ToS was not read directly (only third-party provision summaries); YouTube's ToS clause on downloading/re-uploading others' streams was not retrieved.
- No verified list of individual streamers' public clip-permission policies (international or Brazilian GTA RP streamers) was found.

## 5. Paid clipping campaigns in 2025-2026 (Whop Content Rewards, Clipping, Vyro, streamer/Kick programs): rates, caps, rules

### Takeaway
Paid clipping is now a mainstream marketplace model: a streamer, brand or platform funds a campaign budget and pays clippers per 1,000 verified views posted on the clipper's own accounts, typically around US$1 per 1,000 (observed range roughly US$0.20-6, extremes US$0.50-25 for brand campaigns). Payment depends on campaign rules (minimum views, per-clip caps, required branding, approval, verification window) and stops when the budget is exhausted.

### Cited Findings
- NPR (12 May 2026): Content Rewards and Vyro are marketplaces where agencies post clip-for-cash campaigns; examples include US$1 per 1,000 views for MLB game clips, US$25 per 1,000 for an AI startup's product clips, and Polymarket at US$0.50 per 1,000 with a US$70,000 budget. — [NPR via Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2026-05-12/the-clipping-economy-how-short-form-video-clippers-are-overrunning-the-internet)
- Scale of the workforce: Emrah Bayraktar (Belgium) runs a network of 40,000 freelance clippers, mostly aged 16-24; Cluely hired more than 700 clippers; one 19-year-old clipper reports about US$4,000/month; the agency Clipping (co-founder Anthony Fujiwara) is named: "If algorithms pivot, the clippers pivot." — [NPR via Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2026-05-12/the-clipping-economy-how-short-form-video-clippers-are-overrunning-the-internet)
- Platforms both boost clips and "crack down on pages that post many duplicate clips, which can look spammy or bot-like." — [NPR via Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2026-05-12/the-clipping-economy-how-short-form-video-clippers-are-overrunning-the-internet)
- Whop Content Rewards mechanics (first-party docs): the campaign owner sets a reward rate per 1,000 views, a minimum payout a clip must earn before review, and a maximum payout per clip; Whop's example is a US$3 rate with a US$6 minimum (about 2,000 views to qualify). — [Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/growth-marketing/content-rewards)
- Content Rewards' own guide: most filled campaigns sit between US$0.80 and US$2.00 per 1,000; unsettled payouts return to the campaign if a clip is rejected after approval (including for fake views) or the account is banned. — [Content Rewards, payouts explained](https://contentrewards.com/articles/clipping-payouts-explained)
- Vendor-observed ranges (figures "checked August 2026", no external sources): low US$0.20-0.50, median US$0.80-1.50 (marketplace average about US$1), outliers US$3-6; trackers count verified views within a tracking window, and 20-30% of views typically fail verification (late submission, rule rejection, duplicates, deleted posts, exhausted budgets). — [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/clipping/clipper-pay-rates)
- Other vendor-reported data points: a Roobet campaign at US$1.50 per 1,000 with a US$250k budget; a "Kick Clipping" campaign on Clipping at up to US$0.40 per 1,000 with a 100,000-view minimum; N3on direct payroll working out to US$0.40-0.50 per 1,000; Streampal paying clippers 75% of the campaign rate; streamer bounty programs (Roshtein, Trainwreckstv) at US$1-5 per 1,000; a blog reports a 10% Whop fee and 10-day hold. All from vendor blogs, unverified. — [OpenClip guide](https://openclip.app/guides/whop-clipping-guide); [autoclip](https://autoclip.dev/blog/kick-clip-channel-monetization-2026); [clipaffiliates](https://www.clipaffiliates.com/blog/clipping-cpm-rates-2026); [clipspeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/whop-content-rewards-clipping-guide.html)
- Clavicular told Business of TV his clippers get US$30 per 1,000 views (single self-reported claim, an outlier). — [Business of TV](https://businessoftv.substack.com/p/clippers-and-view-botting-big-social)
- Clavicular said (Iced Coffee Hour, reported 30 Jul 2026) that Kick spent close to US$700,000 on his clipping in one month, that his clipping budget is "unlimited", that "They'll fluctuate the CPM based on the spend", and that "Everyone on Kick" gets this; clips carry his kick.com branding and run on TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Dexerto notes no independent verification or Kick comment. — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/kick/clavicular-says-kick-spent-nearly-700000-promoting-his-clips-in-a-single-month-3393030/)
- Conflicting reports on who pays in the "Kick clipping program": some coverage says Kick pays clippers directly; FindClout reported that as of Sep 2026 it could not find a published platform-wide Kick rate and that such programs are usually run by streamers or organizations. — [FindClout](https://findclout.com/blog/kick-clipping-program); [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/en/blog/kick-pays-clippers-directly-2026)
- Critique: Lou Paskalis calls the arbitrage "a perfectly terrible problem" (middlemen profit while originals lose monetization); Business of TV links clipping to view-botting concerns. — [NPR via Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2026-05-12/the-clipping-economy-how-short-form-video-clippers-are-overrunning-the-internet); [Business of TV](https://businessoftv.substack.com/p/clippers-and-view-botting-big-social)
- Brazil: cut.pro runs "campeonatos de cortes" (free to enter; each publishes minimum views, per-video cap, accepted networks and deadline before sign-up) and describes a championship as a collective authorization with rules. — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/how-to-make-money-clipping-2026); [cut.pro championships](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/championships)
- X ended its old revenue share and its new Original Content Rewards excludes "substancialmente reproduzido" content and downloaded-and-reposted videos (dates conflict in the source: 8 Aug vs 7/8 Sep 2026). — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/reposts-dont-pay-x-meta-youtube-2026)

### Inferences
- Campaign pay does not require platform monetization, so it sidesteps YPP reused-content and TikTok originality rules; however campaign clips are by design near-duplicates posted by many clippers, which raises the risk of spam/duplicate-content suppression on the accounts.
- Most well-funded streamer campaigns are tied to Kick and to gambling sponsors; a Brazilian clipper must filter those out given the betting-ad ban (section 7).
- Realistic earnings maths: at US$1 per 1,000 with a 20-30% verification haircut, 1M posted views yields roughly US$700-800 before marketplace fees and per-clip caps.

- No first-party rate card from Kick; no verified list of streamers currently running programs with their exact rules. Named-streamer rates come from vendor blogs or self-reports.

## 6. Copyright risks: the stream, the game, in-game licensed music (GTA radio), background music; Content ID on Shorts and TikTok muting

### Takeaway
A clip stacks three layers of rights: the streamer's broadcast (needs the streamer's authorization), the game footage (Rockstar tolerates fan gameplay videos under a discretionary policy, not a licence for commercial exploitation) and any music (GTA radio or background tracks, which neither the streamer nor Rockstar's video policy clears). On YouTube, a Content ID claim on a Short longer than one minute blocks it outright; claims are not strikes, while strikes come from formal removal requests.

### Cited Findings
- Rockstar's "Policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar Games material" (latest version found dated 10 Jan 2025, per search excerpt): Rockstar is happy for fans to upload their gameplay footage; the policy targets occasional, non-commercial use by individual fans; isolated cutscene uploads will be removed; Rockstar reserves the right to remove content case by case and change the policy at any time. (Page could not be fetched directly; summary from search excerpt.) — [Rockstar Support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/7bNaeoMFTV0iUDGhStTXvz/policy-on-posting-copyrighted-material)
- Analyses note Rockstar's guidance gives no blanket soundtrack licence to creators; licensed music heard in gameplay can trigger claims even when the gameplay itself is allowed; a narrow GTA V exception is reported for radio tracks in Rockstar Editor videos on YouTube (2024 analysis, unverified against the primary page). — [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/gta-vi-music-what-creators-can-safely-use-in-videos-and-streams); [Attack of the Fanboy](https://attackofthefanboy.com/news/rockstars-policy-sharing-gta-gameplay-youtube/)
- YouTube: "Shorts longer than one minute that have an active Content ID claim, regardless of the policy, will be blocked on YouTube"; these claims do not produce a copyright strike. — [YouTube Help, Content ID claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276?hl=en); [YouTube Help, music eligibility for Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13486873?hl=en)
- Copyright strikes result from a valid copyright removal request, not from Content ID claims; claims usually do not affect channel standing. — [YouTube Help, Content ID claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276?hl=en)
- Music added through the Shorts audio library generates a special, non-disputable claim invisible to creators; music brought in from outside the library is subject to standard claims and removal requests. — [YouTube Help, music eligibility for Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13486873?hl=en)
- Non-music third-party content does not get a share of the Shorts pool "even when a Content ID monetize policy is set"; a Short with music tracks shares its associated revenue with music partners. — [YouTube Help, Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- TikTok may mute, remove or disqualify from rewards videos with "unauthorized uses of music". — [TikTok CRP terms (Brazil)](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-br/en)
- Brazilian law as summarized by a vendor blog: Lei 9.610/98 art. 29 requires "autorização prévia e expressa do autor" for partial or full reproduction and editing; art. 4 says copyright deals are interpreted restrictively; art. 50 says assignment is "sempre por escrito". — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/ask-permission-to-clip-streamer-2026); statute at [Planalto](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9610.htm)
- Twitch precedent: music inside clips leads to muting (Audible Magic) or deletion plus strikes under DMCA notices. — [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2020/06/08/twitch-dmca-takedowns-tos-changes/)

### Inferences
- Practical risk ranking for GTA clips: (1) music (radio stations, songs played by the streamer) is the most likely automated trigger; (2) the streamer or their agency issuing removal requests is the route to actual strikes; (3) Rockstar itself rarely targets ordinary gameplay but its policy is discretionary and framed as non-commercial.
- Keeping Shorts at or under 60 seconds reduces the automatic block risk from Content ID claims; muting or replacing game-radio audio in the edit removes the main claim source. TikTok Creator Rewards, by contrast, requires more than 60 seconds, so the two platforms pull in opposite directions on length.
- An authorization from the streamer should be in writing and cover scope, platforms and takedown-on-request, since Brazilian law reads permissions narrowly.

- YouTube's copyright-strike specifics (three strikes, 90-day expiry, Copyright School) were not retrieved in this session and should be confirmed from YouTube Help before being stated.

## 7. Brazil-specific aspects: official "canais de cortes" models, legal and tax notes

### Takeaway
In Brazil the established pattern is official or authorized cortes channels (e.g., "Cortes do Flow [OFICIAL]") plus fan channels operating under Discord rules or individual agreements; financial terms are private and no reliable published revenue-split percentages were found. A major 2026 legal change matters directly: MP 1.394 (25 Sep 2026) bans betting operations and their advertising, so clips containing betting promotion or campaigns funded by betting sponsors are off-limits while it is in force.

### Cited Findings
- Official cortes channels exist for major shows (e.g., "Cortes do Flow [OFICIAL]", "Groselha Talk - Cortes [OFICIAL]"); an explainer describes the usual logic as the cortes channel earning from views/subscribers while the original earns from referral back to its videos, and cites a fan channel authorized to reproduce Flow material, without financial details. — [vidIQ channel stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC3uYvpJ3J6oNoNYRXfZXjEw); [invideo blog](https://invideo.io/pt/blog/canal-de-cortes-no-youtube-como-criar-um-e-comecar-a-ganhar-dinheiro/)
- Agreement formats described for Brazil: no payment (credit + link); split of the cortes accounts' monetization ("Funciona quando as contas já monetizam"); fixed fee per package paid by the creator; championship paying by performance. The source states there is no official table and gives no percentages. — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/ask-permission-to-clip-streamer-2026)
- Recommended written terms: what can be clipped, where, credit (@ and link), frequency/term, money, removal on request (48 hours cited as reasonable), prohibitions (out-of-context cuts, AI voice/face montage, third-party ads without approval, betting content), and what happens to accounts/videos at the end. — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/ask-permission-to-clip-streamer-2026)
- MP 1.394/2026, published 25 Sep 2026, prohibits the operation, offer, intermediation and advertising of fixed-odds betting nationwide; art. 16 bans communication, advertising, marketing and sponsorship linked to bets in physical or digital media, with an exception for previously published content where betting advertising is incidental. — [Agência Senado](https://www12.senado.leg.br/noticias/materias/2026/09/28/mp-proibe-bets-e-determina-encerramento-das-operacoes-em-30-dias); [Nosso Meio](https://nossomeio.com.br/a-mp-das-bets-e-o-fim-da-publicidade-de-apostas/)
- The MP is valid for 60 days, extendable once (up to 120), and lapses if Congress does not convert it (reported possible expiry around March 2027); prison penalties for promoters belong to a parallel bill, not the MP. — [Correio da Manhã](https://www.correiodamanha.com.br/politica/2026/09/322903-mp-que-proibe-bets-pode-perder-validade-em-marco-de-2027.html); [Garrastazu Advogados](https://garrastazu.adv.br/a-proibicao-das-bets-ja-vale)
- A Brazilian vendor blog also states that paid political content via clip competitions is prohibited by the Electoral Justice during election periods. — [cut.pro](https://cut.pro/pt-BR/blog/how-to-make-money-clipping-2026)
- Tax/payment notes from TikTok's Brazil terms: USD rewards may be converted to Real subject to IOF; the creator is solely responsible for taxes. — [TikTok CRP terms (Brazil)](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-br/en)
- Portuguese-language coverage confirms the 2027 YPP doubling of requirements for new creators. — [Canaltech](https://canaltech.com.br/internet-e-redes-sociais/youtube-muda-regras-e-dificulta-monetizacao-de-novos-canais-veja-exigencias); [TNH1](https://www.tnh1.com.br/noticia/nid/youtube-dobra-exigencia-para-novos-criadores-lucrarem-com-videos/)

### Inferences
- Since many international paid clipping campaigns are funded by gambling brands (Roobet, Stake-linked Kick streamers), a Brazil-based clipper should treat those campaigns and any clip showing betting promotion as legally risky under MP 1.394, at least until Congress decides its fate.
- Brazilian streamer/cortes arrangements are negotiated privately; the report should present split percentages as unknown rather than quote a "standard".
